Book one: Meet Daddy Wizard

Hello! My name is Shayla, and I am five years old. Well, five and three-quarters, nearly. I live in Bentleigh with a secret. A really, REALLY big secret. Can you keep a secret? Good. Come closer...

My dad is a wizard.

I know what you're thinking. Wizards have long beards and pointy hats and live in tall stone towers. Not my dad. My dad drinks about a hundred Coke No Sugars a day. He brings me a banana muffin after school, he calls me "knucklehead," and he tells the worst jokes in the whole universe.

But when nobody is looking, he wiggles his fingers, whispers some words, and... ZING! Magic happens. Well. Something happens.

Because here's the second secret. My dad is a little bit rubbish at magic. Once he tried a spell to fold the washing. The socks didn't fold. They got up and danced a conga line around the lounge room.

Once he tried a spell to help me ride my bike without training wheels. "Balance-o wobble-o!" he said. My bike didn't wobble. It floated up, up, up and balanced on the roof of the house! (Actually, that one was pretty great. The view was amazing.)

And once, ONLY once, he tried to magic up pancakes. We don't talk about the frogs. There were a LOT of frogs.

I'm not a wizard. Not yet, anyway. Dad says wizard magic only shows up when you're grown up. But I'm learning something even more useful. I'm learning why Dad's spells go wrong.

I keep it all in my secret notebook. It's light pink, with a lock on the front and a fairy princess on the cover. Inside are all of Dad's spells, every mistake, and my very best ideas for fixing them.

Here is what I've found out so far: One: Dad RUSHES. Spells hate being rushed. Two: Dad mixes up his words. (Never say "broom" when you mean "bloom." Trust me.) Three: Dad forgets the most important magic word of all. Can you guess it? It's "please."

So when Dad's magic goes wonky, and it always goes wonky, that's where I come in. I grab my notebook, I put on my thinking face, and I fix it.

Sometimes it's easy. Sometimes it's very, VERY hard. And sometimes... just sometimes... when I'm fixing things, my fingertips go fizzy and warm. Like tiny sparklers. Dad hasn't noticed yet. I haven't told him. (That's secret number three. Shhh.)

People ask me if it's hard having a wizard for a dad. I tell them the truth. His magic is silly. His spells are wobbly. His jokes are TERRIBLE.

But every night, when he tucks me in, he doesn't need any magic words at all. He just says, "Love you, Shayla." And somehow, that's the strongest magic in the whole world.

And I say back, "Best magic team in the world." Because that's what we are. Dad and me.

So, do you want to see what happens when Dad tries to magic the house clean before Nana comes to visit? Turn to the next book... and hold on tight. The vacuum cleaner is about to run away. The end... for now.
