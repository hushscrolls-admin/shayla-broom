Book four: Dad Shrinks Himself

It was Friday night, which meant movie night. Popcorn: ready. Blanket: ready. Billy: ready. The TV remote: … gone.

We looked everywhere. Under the cushions. Behind the piano. In the fridge. (Dad once left his keys in there.) "It's under the couch," said Dad. "I can see it. I just can't reach it."

Dad lay on his tummy and stretched. And stretched. "Shay," he grunted, "I think this is a job for… MAGIC." I grabbed my light pink notebook. Here we go again.

He wiggled his fingers fast. "Small-o, reach-o, tiny-tiny-o!" I checked my notebook. Rushing? Yes. Mixed-up words? Yep. "Please"? You know already.

POP! Dad was gone. "Dad?" "Down here!" said a teeny-tiny voice.

I look down. There was Dad, standing next to my sock, about as tall as Billy. Maybe shorter. He was still wearing his holey T-shirt.

Billy looked at Dad. Dad looked at Billy. For the first time ever, Billy was the big one.

Then… knock, knock, knock. "Hi, sweetheart! It's Mummy!" Oh no. Mummy was dropping off my library bag, and Dad was the size of a fish finger.

"Quick!" squeaked Dad. "Hide me!" I scooped him up and popped him in the pocket of my notebook. Only his head poked out.

Mummy came in. "Where's your dad?" "Um… he's in the toilet," I said. In my pocket, Dad gave a tiny sneeze.

"Bless you," said Mummy, looking at me. I wiped my nose, just in case. Billy stared at the ceiling, very innocent.

Mummy gave me a big hug and a kiss on the head. "See you Sunday, Shayla. Tell Dad he's got popcorn on his shirt." I didn't tell her he was in my pocket.

As soon as the door shut, I took Dad out and put him on the coffee table. "Dad," I said, "you have to undo it. Slowly. Properly. And nicely."

Dad took a big breath. He wiggled his tiny fingers, gently. "Me, please, back to normal." POP! There was Dad, full size, sitting on the coffee table. It made a very worried creak.

And in his hand? The TV remote. "Found it under the couch," he grinned, "while I was little." I wrote in my notebook: Rule four. Sometimes being small helps.

That night, after the movie, Dad tucked me in. "Thanks for hiding me, Shayla. Love you." "Love you too," I said. On the shelf, Billy looked a tiny bit sad about being small again. The end.
