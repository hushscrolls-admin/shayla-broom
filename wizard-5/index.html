(Not Very Good) Wizard

Book five: Shayla's Turn

It was Saturday morning, and Dad was still in bed. His nose was red. His eyes were droopy. He had a box of tissues on his tummy like a tiny white mountain.

"Good borning, Shay," he croaked. Then his voice went squeak… and then it went nothing at all. Dad had lost his voice.

Dad tried to wiggle his fingers and whisper a feel-better spell. But no words came out. Just a big, wet ATCHOO! No voice means no magic words. And no magic words means no magic.

I looked at Dad. I looked at Billy. Then I put my hands on my hips. "Don't worry, Dad," I said. "Today, I'm the wizard."

Dad's eyebrows went up, up, up. He opened his mouth to say something. All that came out was a teeny-tiny squeak. I grabbed my light pink notebook. My fingertips were already fizzy.

First, Dad needed soup. I stood in the kitchen and copied Dad's wiggle exactly. Well… nearly exactly. "Soup-o, hot-o, yummy-o!"

The pot bubbled. It burbled. It smelled very… sweet. I had made banana muffin soup. Dad had one spoonful. He gave me a thumbs-up and a very brave smile.

Next, I fixed Dad's holey T-shirt. "Holes-o, gone-o!" I wiggled. ZING! The holes were gone. But now his T-shirt was covered in rainbows. Big ones. Sparkly ones. Dad looked down and blinked.

Then Billy wanted to help too. "Billy, be my magic helper," I said, and I gave a tiny practice wiggle. Billy opened her beak to quack. "ROOOAAARRR!"

Billy roared like a lion. Dad jumped so high his tissues went everywhere. Billy looked VERY surprised. She had never been scary before.

I flopped onto the end of Dad's bed. "My magic is all sideways," I said. "Soup. T-shirt. Billy. Everything!" Dad patted the blanket. Then he held up his hand and wiggled his fingers, very slowly, so I could see.

Oh. Oh. Dad wiggles his pointer finger and his pinkie. I had been wiggling my thumb and my middle finger! I wrote in my notebook: Rule five. Wiggle the RIGHT fingers.

I took a big breath. I wiggled the right fingers, slowly, properly and nicely. "Billy, please, quack again." Billy opened her beak. "Quack!" A good, normal, Billy-sized quack.

But Dad still had his cold. And I knew a spell couldn't fix that one. So I got my favourite book, climbed under the blanket, and curled up right next to him.

I read him the whole story, doing all the voices, because he couldn't. Halfway through, Dad was smiling. By the end, he was snoring. (Very loudly. Like a sleepy lion.)

That night, Dad's voice came back, just a little bit. "Best wizard in the world," he whispered. "Love you, Shayla." "Love you too," I said. "Even in your rainbow T-shirt." On the end of the bed, Billy gave a tiny, happy quack. The end.
