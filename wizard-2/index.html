Book two: The Runaway Vacuum

It was Saturday morning, and Dad was running around the house like a headless chook. "Nana's coming at twelve o'clock!" he said. "And this house looks like a tornado had a party in it!"

He wasn't wrong. There were Lego bricks on the floor, cereal on the couch, and a sock sitting on top of the piano. (Nobody knows how that got there.)

Dad looked at the clock. Then he looked at the mess. Then he looked at me. "Shay," he said, "I think this is a job for... MAGIC." I grabbed my light pink notebook. This was going to be bad.

Dad rolled up his sleeves and pointed at the vacuum cleaner. "Vacuum-o, clean-o, quick-o-quick-o!" he shouted. I checked my notebook. Rushing? Yes. Mixed-up words? Probably. "Please"? Nope.

The vacuum cleaner shivered. It wiggled. Its hose stood up like an elephant's trunk. Then it went VROOOOM!

It sucked up the Lego. It sucked up the cereal. It sucked up the sock from the piano. "It's working!" Dad cheered. Then it sucked up the TV remote, Dad's glass of Coke No Sugar, and Billy!

"STOP!" yelled Dad. But the vacuum did not stop. It zoomed out the front door, bumped down the steps, and raced off down the street, its cord flapping behind it like a tail.

We ran after it. Past the IGA. Past the park. Past a very surprised man walking his dog. The vacuum sucked up a pile of autumn leaves and burped them out again in a big crunchy cloud.

"What do we do?" puffed Dad. "Nana will be here in twenty minutes!" I opened my notebook and put on my thinking face.

Rule one: Dad RUSHES. Rule two: Dad mixes up his words. Rule three: Dad forgets to say "please." "Dad," I said, "you have to undo it. Slowly. Properly. And nicely."

Dad took a big breath. He stood very still. He wiggled his fingers gently, the way you'd pat a puppy. "Vacuum, please, come home," he said softly.

The vacuum stopped. It turned around. Then, with a polite little pop, it spat out Billy, a bit fluffier than before, and trundled back up the street to park itself in the cupboard. I gave Billy the biggest hug.

But the house was still a mess. And it was 11:55. Dad flopped onto the couch. "We'll never get it clean in time." That's when my fingertips went fizzy. Just a little. Just for a second.

I pointed at the Lego, and every single brick hopped back into its box. Click, click, click. Dad was looking at the clock. He didn't see a thing. (Secret number three is still a secret.)

Ding-dong! It was Nana. She looked around the tidy lounge room and smiled. "My goodness, you two have been busy!" Dad winked at me. I winked back.

That night, Dad tucked me in. "Thanks for saving the day, Shayla," he whispered. "Love you." "Best magic team in the world," I said. Downstairs, very quietly, the vacuum cleaner gave a happy little vroom. The end.
