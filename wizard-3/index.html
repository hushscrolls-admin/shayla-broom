Book three: The Bike That Rode Itself

It was Sunday, and it was a very big day. Today I was going to ride my bike with NO training wheels.

Dad unscrewed the little wheels and held them up like a trophy. "Look at that, Shay. You're a big kid now." My tummy did a wobbly flip.

At the park, I pushed off. Wobble. Wobble. Crash. Right into the grass. "That's okay!" said Dad. "Everybody falls at first."

I tried again. Wobble. Wobble. Crash. Billy watched from the bench. She looked worried.

Dad looked left. Dad looked right. Then he whispered, "Maybe… a tiny bit of help." I grabbed my light pink notebook. Oh no.

He wiggled his fingers fast. "Balance-o, wheel-o, zoom-o-zoom-o!" I checked my notebook. Rushing? Yes. Mixed-up words? Definitely. "Please"? Nope.

My bike gave a little shiver. Then it took off, pedalling all by itself, with me hanging on and my feet flying!

We zoomed past the swings. We zoomed around the duck pond. A duck quacked at us, very rudely.

"Hang on, Shay!" puffed Dad, running behind. "I'm coming!" He was not very fast. His shoelace was undone.

The bike did a wheelie. Then it did a loop around the slide. I squeezed the handlebars and shut my eyes.

"Dad!" I yelled. "Slowly! Properly! NICELY!" Dad stopped. He took a big breath and wiggled his fingers gently. "Bike, please, slow down."

The bike slowed down… and down… and stopped right next to Billy on the bench, as polite as anything.

Dad flopped onto the grass, puffing. "Sorry, Shay. No more magic bikes." I looked at my bike. I looked at the long, flat path. "Dad… can I try by myself?"

Wobble. Wobble… pedal. Pedal, pedal, PEDAL. I didn't fall! I was riding all by myself, with no magic at all!

"You did it!" Dad cheered, jumping up and down like a kangaroo. "That was all you, Shayla!" And it was. (I checked my fingertips. Not even a fizz.)

That night, Dad tucked me in. "Proudest dad in the whole world," he whispered. "Love you." "Love you too," I said. Out in the hallway, my bike gave a tiny happy ding. The end.
